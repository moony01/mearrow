The night of February 22, 2026, XG wrapped their world tour performance in Nagoya. The seven members took their final bow and walked offstage. Hours later, in a hotel in the same city, police executed a search.

The person arrested was Simon Jakops — CEO of XGALX and the man who built XG from the ground up.

## The Nagoya Hotel Raid

On February 23, the Tokyo Metropolitan Police searched a hotel in Aichi Prefecture, Nagoya. Officers discovered multiple bags of cocaine and dried cannabis. Four people were arrested on suspicion of violating Japan's Narcotics Control Act, including Simon Jakops (real name Junho Sakai, 39) and K-hip hop producer Chancellor (real name Kim Jung-seung).

### Who Is Simon Jakops?

Simon is a Korean-Japanese former idol who founded XGALX in 2017 and launched XG in 2022 — making them the first Japanese girl group to meaningfully break into the global K-pop scene. He personally oversaw every aspect of XG's music, choreography, and visual concept. Across the industry, he was called the "soul" of the group.

The arrest sent immediate shockwaves through the XG fandom. The timing made it hit harder: it happened just hours after the group had performed in front of thousands.

## The Day After: XG Draws a Line

On February 24, XG issued an official statement via their social media channels.

> "We sincerely apologize for the concern and inconvenience caused to our fans and related parties due to the recent reports. A police investigation is currently underway, and we are fully cooperating with authorities. XG has **absolutely no involvement** in this matter."

The members added personally: "We are also processing this sudden development with great shock and confusion. XG will continue to walk forward as XG."

On February 27, XGALX announced that their ongoing 2nd world tour "THE CORE" would proceed as scheduled — covering remaining Japan dates in March and April, followed by Asia, North America, Europe, Australia, and Latin America.

It read as a declaration of resolve. Five days later, a second bomb dropped.

## Five Days Later: 'The Abuse Was Routine'

Around February 28, multiple sources began reporting on internal allegations. Per reports by Asian Junkie and Koreaboo:

- Simon allegedly engaged in **verbal and physical intimidation** of staff — slamming his fists on desks and throwing plastic bottles to startle employees.
- Over a two-year period, **at least 10 employees** resigned or requested transfers due to his behavior.
- After XG's debut, his drinking reportedly worsened, with club and bar tabs billed to the **company card**.
- Questions were also raised about opaque credit attribution and revenue distribution practices inside XGALX.

What had appeared to be a personal legal matter began to look like the visible edge of something systemic.

## MEARROW's Take: Can XG Survive Without Its Architect?

XG holds a genuinely unusual position in K-pop history: seven women signed to a Japanese label, performing English-language hip-hop and R&B with a growing global fanbase. That identity was designed, from the ground up, by Simon Jakops.

Two questions now define their trajectory.

**First, can XGALX fill the creative vacuum?** Simon wasn't just a CEO — he was the hands-on creative director who shaped XG's music, visuals, and artistic direction. A prolonged absence creates real uncertainty about the group's identity. In K-pop, this pattern has played out before: when the key creative architect disappears, the group's direction often drifts.

**Second, do the abuse allegations escalate legally?** Drug charges alone can result in extended detention in Japan. If the workplace misconduct claims become formal proceedings, XGALX faces pressure not just creatively but structurally. A company built around one person is vulnerable in ways that a more distributed operation is not.

When XG's seven members said "XG will continue to walk forward as XG," it may not have simply been reassurance for fans. It may have been a public declaration that they intend to stand without their founder.

## The Real Test Starts Now

XG's first performances without Simon Jakops are scheduled for March in Japan. What the seven members choose to show on that stage — and how — will likely define what XG actually is.

The question fans are left with: **Was XG's soul Simon Jakops, or was it always the seven of them?**
