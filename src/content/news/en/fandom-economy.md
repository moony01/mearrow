## Fandoms: From Consumers to Shareholders

In the K-POP industry, fandoms are no longer passive consumers. They move systematically for the artist growth and play the role of "de facto shareholders," even influencing the stock prices of agencies.

### 1. Album Hauls and Photocard Culture

Physical album sales remain the most definitive indicator of a fandom's firepower. The culture of "album hauls"—buying dozens or hundreds of albums to get fan sign event lottery tickets and random photocards—is a unique economic phenomenon in K-POP. While criticized for environmental issues, it is undeniably a powerful revenue model sustaining the music market.

### 2. Birthday Cafes and Subway Ads

The culture of "Birthday Cafes," where fans voluntarily rent and decorate cafes for an artist's birthday or debut anniversary, also impacts local commercial districts. Billboard ads at major subway stations in Hongdae and Gangnam, Seoul, have become essential pilgrimage courses for idol fandoms. This is a prime example of fandom culture expanding into the offline real economy.

### 3. Donations and Positive Influence

Recently, a culture of donating in the artist's name instead of giving gifts (tributes) has taken root. Fandom energy is evolving into creating social value through rice wreaths, forest creation, and flood relief donations. This not only enhances the artist's image but also has a positive impact on society as a whole.
