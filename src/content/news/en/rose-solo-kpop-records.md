Rosé broke every ceiling a female K-pop solo artist could reach. She did it in six months.

From the moment "APT." with Bruno Mars landed on Spotify on October 18, 2024, to the moment she held two trophies at the iHeartRadio Music Awards on March 26, 2026 — what happened in between demanded a complete rewrite of K-pop solo history.

## APT. — 1 Billion in 100 Days

### 100 Million in 7 Days, 1 Billion in 100

"APT." hit 100 million Spotify streams in just seven days — the fastest ever for a female K-pop solo artist. One hundred days later, that number crossed one billion.

On the Billboard Hot 100, it peaked at No. 3 and stayed on the chart for 45 weeks. On the Billboard Global 200, it spent **12 consecutive weeks at No. 1**, nearly doubling the previous record for an Asian artist set by Jungkook's "Seven" (7 weeks). It was also the first song by an Asian act to top both the Global 200 and Global Excl. US charts **simultaneously for 8 weeks**.

No Korean solo female artist had ever posted these numbers in pop chart history. Bruno Mars brought massive reach as a collaborator, but Rosé led the songwriting and vocal direction — a detail that matters when measuring artistic ownership of this success.

## rosie — Billboard 200 No. 3 and a Guinness Record

### The Highest K-pop Female Solo Album on Billboard 200

Released on December 6, 2024, debut studio album "rosie" moved 102,000 units in its first week and debuted at No. 3 on the Billboard 200. That ranking became the **highest-ever position for a K-pop female solo artist** on the chart — officially recognized by Guinness World Records.

On Spotify, the album logged 179.3 million first-week streams, the largest opening for any female K-pop act in history. On the United World Chart, Rosé topped both the albums and singles charts simultaneously, making her the **first Asian solo artist** to achieve this milestone.

## Grammy Night — The First K-pop Soloist to Open That Stage

### 3 Nominations, Big Four Candidacy, Opening Performance

February 1, 2026. The 68th Grammy Awards. Rosé took the stage alongside Bruno Mars as the **opening act** — the first Grammy performance ever by a K-pop solo artist.

"APT." earned three nominations:

- **Record of the Year**
- **Song of the Year**
- **Best Pop Duo/Group Performance**

Record of the Year and Song of the Year are Grammy "Big Four" categories. No K-pop artist had ever been nominated for a Big Four award as a lead artist before. The wins went to Kendrick Lamar (Record of the Year), Billie Eilish (Song of the Year), and Cynthia Erivo & Ariana Grande (Best Pop Duo), but earning nominations across three categories redrew the map of what K-pop soloists could achieve on music's biggest stage.

## iHeartRadio Double Crown — Two More 'First in K-pop' Titles

### March 26: Another Historic Night

The most recent milestone landed just three days ago. At the 2026 iHeartRadio Music Awards on March 26, held at the Dolby Theatre in Los Angeles, Rosé walked away with two awards:

- **Best Collaboration** (first K-pop artist ever to win)
- **K-pop Artist of the Year** (first female K-pop artist to win)

iHeartRadio operates the largest radio network in the United States. A K-pop act had never taken the Best Collaboration trophy, and no woman had ever been named K-pop Artist of the Year at the ceremony. At the Grammys and at iHeartRadio, Rosé keeps writing the word "first" next to her name.

## MEARROW's Take — The Solo Era Has Truly Arrived

### Not Escaping a Group's Shadow, But Drawing a New Map

In K-pop, solo activity has long been treated as a byproduct of group promotions — filler between comebacks or a personal branding exercise. A solo career outperforming the group was considered an anomaly.

Rosé changed the equation. "APT."'s 12 weeks at No. 1 on the Billboard Global 200 exceeds any single-song record from BLACKPINK as a group. While "rosie"'s No. 3 on the Billboard 200 falls short of BLACKPINK's "Born Pink" (No. 1), the 102,000 first-week units and Guinness recognition represent an unprecedented scale for a K-pop female solo artist.

What makes this story structurally significant is that these results came from **the artist's own creative direction and global network**, not label resource allocation. Rosé co-wrote and co-produced "rosie," and her partnership with Bruno Mars began through personal connections. This is proof of concept for an artist succeeding outside the agency system — a paradigm the industry has rarely seen.

## Is There a Ceiling Left to Break?

Six months. One billion streams. Billboard 200 No. 3. Grammy opening. iHeartRadio double crown. Guinness.

In 2026, BLACKPINK continues full-scale activity following their "DEADLINE" EP. Rosé is both a BLACKPINK member and, now, the benchmark for K-pop solo history itself.

The only question left: is there a ceiling still above her — or is she already standing above it?
