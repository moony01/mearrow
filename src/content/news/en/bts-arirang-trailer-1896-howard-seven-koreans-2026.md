On March 13, 2026, BTS uploaded a 2-minute-49-second animated video to their official YouTube channel BANGTANTV. The title: **'ARIRANG Animation Trailer: What is your love song?'**

Within 24 hours, ARMY across every platform stopped everything. This wasn't a standard album teaser. It was a 130-year historical epic — and fans are still piecing it together.

## 1896: What Happened In Washington D.C.

### Seven Korean Students At Howard University

The trailer opens with this text on screen:

> *"This video was inspired by the story of seven young Koreans as documented in The Washington Post on May 8, 1896 ('Seven Koreans at Howard'), some of whom captured the first known audio recordings of Koreans in Washington, D.C., on July 24."*

May 8, 1896. The Washington Post ran an article titled "Seven Koreans at Howard," reporting that a group of seven Korean (then Joseon) students visited Howard University — a historically Black university — and performed, drawing enthusiastic applause from "dozens of students."

Then, on July 24 of that same year, some of those students made **wax cylinder recordings** of Korean songs — including what is believed to be the earliest known audio recording of "Arirang." According to Library of Congress records, the singers included Ahn Jeong-sik, Lee Hee-Cheol, and Son Rong. Their voices were preserved on six wax cylinders — the **first known audio recordings of Korean voices** in American history.

At the time, Korea stood on the eve of Japanese occupation. These seven students, in the middle of a foreign country, sang to ensure they would not be forgotten.

## What The Animated Trailer Actually Shows

### A Red Thread Across 130 Years

The animation opens in 1896. Seven young men in formal Western suits gather around a phonograph as a melody begins to play. The camera lingers on the recording moment, then jumps forward in time. The final scene: seven men, standing together again. The number seven repeats — deliberately.

Throughout the trailer, a **red thread** appears and reappears. In East Asian tradition, a red thread connects people bound by fate. Here, it binds 1896 to 2026.
