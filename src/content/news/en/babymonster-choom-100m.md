`BABYMONSTER CHOOM 100M` sounds like a clean victory headline. It is not that simple. On May 18 at about 2:50 p.m. KST, the music video for `CHOOM` crossed 100 million YouTube views, roughly 13 days and 21 hours after its May 4 release. The obvious read is that BABYMONSTER has another viral trophy. The sharper read is that YG Entertainment now has a bigger problem: every new BABYMONSTER campaign is being measured against a speed curve most young groups cannot survive. ## BABYMONSTER CHOOM 100M Is Not Just A YouTube Flex

### The 13-day clock changed the benchmark

Soompi, Kpopify and Music Mundial all reported the same core milestone: `CHOOM` passed 100 million views on May 18, about 13 days and 21 hours after the video arrived on May 4 at 6 p.m. KST. Soompi also framed it as the second K-pop music video released in 2026 to reach the mark, and the fastest 2026 K-pop MV to do it so far.

That matters because the number is not floating by itself. BABYMONSTER already had a YouTube-heavy identity before this comeback, but `CHOOM` arrived in a more crowded market than the group's earlier breakout clips. May 2026 has been packed with major girl-group releases, reunion storylines, tour announcements and teaser traffic. A 100 million-view sprint inside that noise does not only show that fans clicked. It shows that the campaign kept converting attention after the first-day curiosity faded.

### The milestone is also a catalog signal

The record becomes louder when placed next to BABYMONSTER's broader video history. Soompi reported that `CHOOM` is the group's 11th official music video to reach 100 million views, following earlier clips such as `BATTER UP`, `SHEESH`, `DRIP`, `CLIK CLAK`, `WE GO UP`, `Really Like You` and `PSYCHO`.

That list is the real pressure point. One hit can be explained as a perfect song, a perfect thumbnail, or a perfect algorithmic accident. Eleven official music videos past 100 million views look more like a system. For YG, that is valuable because the company can sell BABYMONSTER as repeatable digital infrastructure. It is also dangerous because repeatability becomes an expectation. The next video is no longer judged against normal rookie standards. It is judged against BABYMONSTER's own machine.

## The Subscriber Surge Makes The Story Harder To Ignore

### Viewers did not only watch; many stayed

The stronger signal may be the subscriber jump around the same window. Soompi and ChosunBiz reported that BABYMONSTER's official YouTube channel passed 12 million subscribers around 6:30 p.m. KST on May 17, with YG saying the channel gained about 250,000 subscribers in the 13 days leading up to that point. That is nearly 20,000 new subscribers per day on average.

Views can be bought, boosted, debated or dismissed by rival fandoms. Subscription growth is harder to wave away because it suggests a portion of casual viewers wanted the next notification. In K-pop's platform economy, that matters more than a single visible count. A subscriber is not guaranteed to buy an album or a ticket, but it gives the label a direct path back into that person's feed when the next performance video, behind-the-scenes clip or tour edit appears.

### The platform behavior fits the live strategy

Allkpop and Fan N Star reported that `CHOOM` topped YouTube's global daily trending music video chart shortly after release, entered the platform's most-viewed-within-24-hours lane, and moved past 15 million views within half a day. Those early signals explain why YG can keep treating BABYMONSTER like a performance-first act rather than a standard rookie content feed.

The group is not only asking fans to stream a music video. It is asking them to imagine the song onstage. That is where `CHOOM` becomes commercially useful: the concept translates into choreography clips, music-show stages, dance practice cuts, short-form challenges and tour anticipation. A view count gets the headline, but performance portability keeps the campaign alive.

## Why This Makes YG's Problem Bigger

### Success raises the cost of every next move

YG's advantage is clear. BABYMONSTER can turn a music video into a global platform event quickly, and the group has enough live branding to connect that attention to the `CHOOM` world tour. The risk is just as clear. A campaign this fast gives the market less patience. If the next clip slows down, if the next single feels smaller, or if tour content does not match the digital promise, the same audience that inflated the numbers can turn the comparison into a burden.

This is the hidden trap of K-pop growth. A young group wants proof that it can scale. Once the proof arrives, the company has to operate as if the scale is permanent. Content gaps feel longer. Music-show stages are judged harder. Ticketing, member health, production design, choreography difficulty and fan communication all become part of the same scoreboard.

### BABYMONSTER is moving from buzz to infrastructure

Hanteo News reported that BABYMONSTER opened additional Seoul concert seats after the three Seoul `CHOOM` shows sold out, and it also cited 387,871 first-day sales for the new mini album. Allkpop separately reported that the Asia and Oceania portion of the tour spans 18 stops, with more regions still expected.

That is the bigger picture behind `100 million in 13 days`. The number is no longer just a brag for MONSTIEZ. It is a demand forecast. YG can use it when selling the group to promoters, platforms, brand partners and markets that want evidence before committing to scale. But evidence cuts both ways. If BABYMONSTER is already being positioned as a touring and video engine, every future release has to feed that engine.

## MEARROW's View On BABYMONSTER CHOOM 100M

### The real win is conversion, not views

MEARROW reads `BABYMONSTER CHOOM 100M` as a conversion story. The group converted a comeback into subscribers, subscribers into repeat video demand, video demand into stronger tour logic, and tour logic back into the myth that BABYMONSTER is built for the stage. That loop is exactly what entertainment companies want from a young global act.

For trainees and aspiring idols, the lesson is blunt. Viral visibility helps, but it is not enough. BABYMONSTER's current momentum works because the group can attach platform numbers to performance readiness. The public sees views. The company sees a live product, a subscriber base, a tour route and a campaign structure that can be repeated if the members keep delivering.

## The Next 100 Million Will Be Harder

### The market will not grade BABYMONSTER gently

The first 100 million views created a headline. The next phase will test whether `CHOOM` can become a durable era rather than a fast statistic. That depends on repeat stages, tour clips, fan edits, follow-up videos and whether casual viewers keep treating BABYMONSTER as more than a YouTube event.

The uncomfortable part for YG is that BABYMONSTER has already made the bar visible. A normal young group would celebrate 100 million views and breathe. BABYMONSTER does not get that luxury anymore. The 13-day clock is now part of the group's story, and every future comeback has to answer the question it created: was `CHOOM` a peak, or was it only the floor?
