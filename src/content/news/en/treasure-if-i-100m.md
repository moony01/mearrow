`TREASURE IF I 100 million views` is not just another YouTube milestone headline. It is a speed signal. [Soompi](https://www.soompi.com/article/1847488wpp/treasures-if-i-becomes-fastest-k-pop-mv-of-2026-to-hit-100-million-views) reported that, according to YG Entertainment, TREASURE’s music video for `IF I` surpassed `100 million` YouTube views on June 13 at about `4:49 p.m. KST`. The same report said the video was released on June 1 at `6 p.m. KST`, meaning it reached the mark in `11 days, 22 hours and 49 minutes` — the fastest K-pop music video of 2026 to do so.

That matters because the number is not floating alone. `IF I` is tied to TREASURE’s fourth mini-album `NEW WAV`, a comeback arriving inside one of the most crowded K-pop months of the year. [Related: June K-pop comeback pileup showed why the calendar became the real fight](//news/june-kpop-comeback-pileup). In that environment, a view count does more than measure curiosity. It shows whether a fandom can create enough repeat motion to keep one song visible while the rest of the market keeps moving.

## TREASURE’s IF I Milestone Is About Speed

### 100 million views in under 12 days changes the scale of the comeback

The clean fact is easy to repeat: `IF I` hit `100 million` views in less than two weeks. The more useful read is what that pace says about the comeback’s operating power. Soompi noted that the video became TREASURE’s fastest music video to pass the mark, and the group’s fourth official music video to do it after `BOY`, `I LOVE YOU` and `JIKJIN`.

That sequence is important. A first viral spike can be treated as a surprise. A fourth official music video crossing the threshold looks more like a system. It means TREASURE is no longer only trying to prove that the group can produce one widely watched clip. The better question is whether the group can now stack milestones quickly enough for each comeback to feel bigger than the last.

For YG, that is the exact kind of signal a boy group needs in 2026. The market is crowded with faster rookie cycles, tighter short-form campaigns and fanbases trained to move immediately. A video record does not guarantee long-term chart dominance, but it proves the campaign created fast behavior. In modern K-pop, fast behavior is often the first layer of durable leverage.

## Why The Black-And-White Performance Hook Worked

### The song gave fans a visual language they could repeat

A milestone like this usually needs more than a good chorus. It needs a visual idea that fans can repeat, edit and explain quickly. [Allkpop](https://www.allkpop.com/article/2026/06/treasures-if-i-music-video-reaches-100-million-views-on-youtube) pointed to the music video’s black-and-white style, hip-hop dance moves and the spread of key dance steps on TikTok and Instagram. That is the important layer: the video did not travel only as a passive watch count. It traveled as a performance object.

That distinction matters for TREASURE because the group’s strongest lane has always been tied to performance intensity. `IF I` did not need to look soft, expensive or lore-heavy to win attention. It needed to look repeatable and forceful enough for clips, challenges and fan edits to do second-stage promotion after release day.

## NEW WAV Is Now Carrying A Bigger Argument

### The YouTube number makes the album rollout harder to ignore

The `100 million` view mark also changes how `NEW WAV` is read. Allkpop reported that the mini-album sold more than `1 million` copies in its first week, reached No. 1 on iTunes in `13` regions, and topped major album charts in Korea and Japan. Those claims need to be read as reported campaign indicators, not as the whole story. Still, they show why the music-video milestone is landing with extra weight.

A strong album figure says the core fandom bought in. A fast video milestone says the visual campaign created ongoing attention. Regional iTunes and album-chart movement say the rollout is not trapped in one domestic lane. None of those alone would settle TREASURE’s 2026 position. Together, they make the comeback feel like a coordinated push rather than one lucky headline.

## The Fandom Test Is Now Different

### TEUME did not just watch; they kept the campaign alive

A fast YouTube milestone is often framed as a fan celebration, and it should be. But from an industry view, it is also a labor test. Fans had to watch, clip, translate, repost, challenge, argue and keep the song visible through the first two weeks. That is not automatic in a month where every fandom is being asked to do the same thing for its own comeback.

This is why the `IF I` number is useful even if someone does not treat view counts as the purest music metric. The count reveals speed, coordination and repeat behavior. It shows that TREASURE’s fanbase could create a visible campaign loop fast enough for media to treat the milestone as news. In a release market where attention decays quickly, that loop can be more valuable than one clean debut-day headline.

The risk is that fast milestones can create faster pressure. Once a group proves it can move at this pace, every next comeback gets compared against the same clock. A slower second week can look like decline, even if the song performs well by normal standards. That is the cost of turning speed into a brand signal.
