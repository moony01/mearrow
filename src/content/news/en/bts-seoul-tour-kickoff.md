On April 9, 2026, BTS opened the `BTS ARIRANG world tour` at Goyang Stadium near Seoul. The headline is not just that the group is back on tour. In heavy rain, a 40,000-plus-capacity stadium still filled up, while merch pickup, fan events, lodging, transit, and food spending spread well beyond the venue. The `BTS Seoul tour kickoff` started not as a single concert, but as a city-scale market event.

## Opening Night Beat the Weather

### More Than 40,000 Seats, Still Full

The Associated Press reported that despite pouring rain on April 9, the stadium packed in a crowd of more than 40,000. Part of that momentum comes from symbolism: this is BTS's first headline world tour launch since the 2021-22 `Permission to Dance on Stage` run. But symbolism alone does not fill a stadium in bad weather. Demand does, and for BTS, that demand had already been locked in long before showtime.

### A Three-Show Window That Sets the Tone

The Goyang opener runs across April 9, 11, and 12. That matters because it turns the kickoff into a multi-day travel event rather than a one-night spike. Fans move between Seoul and Goyang, spreading spending across hotels, restaurants, transit hubs, and retail zones. The first city of a world tour always sets the emotional tone; this one also set the commercial tone.

## The Bigger Money Moves Outside the Venue

### Merch Pickup Is Part of the Business Model

Weverse Shop scheduled on-site pickup for official merch and albums from April 8 through April 12 at the Goyang venue. That sounds logistical, but it is really economic design. Fans arrive early, wait, browse, eat, move between neighborhoods, and turn dead time into spending time. The merch line is not separate from the concert economy. It is one of the engines that enlarges it.

### 'THE CITY SEOUL' Pulled the Tour Into the City

This is where the 2026 rollout looks more sophisticated than a standard stadium run. According to Weverse notices, `BTS THE CITY ARIRANG - SEOUL` began city events on March 20, while the stamp-rally style activation continues through April 19. That means the concert is no longer confined to the venue. Even fans without tickets can still enter the BTS economy through exhibitions, reservations, branded spaces, and citywide participation.

## Why the Revenue Picture Looks Bigger This Time

### A No. 1 Album and a Tour Are Landing Together

AP also noted that `ARIRANG` debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200. That timing matters. When an album hits at the same moment a tour opens, fan spending does not split into separate waves. Streaming, merch, ticket demand, cinema live-viewing, and on-site participation all reinforce one another. The tour is not just promoting the album; the album is feeding the tour's economics in real time.

### 85 Shows Across 34 Cities Means the Template Matters

Yonhap and The Korea Times reported that the `ARIRANG` world tour is launching as an 85-show, 34-city run, which BigHit Music described as the largest tour by a Korean act. The size is impressive, but the more important question is replication. If Goyang proves the model works, then Tokyo, North America, Europe, and the rest of Asia can inherit the same monetization structure with lower execution risk.

## MEARROW's Take on the BTS Seoul Kickoff

### The Real Product Is Demand Translation

MEARROW's view is that the sold-out crowd is only the first layer. BTS already used the free Gwanghwamun comeback concert to restore symbolic momentum. The Goyang kickoff now converts that momentum into paid demand. Add merch pickup, digital viewing, city activations, and travel spillover, and the result is a tour that translates fandom emotion into multiple revenue channels at once. What opened near Seoul on April 9 was not simply a concert. It was a working blueprint for the next phase of K-pop touring.
