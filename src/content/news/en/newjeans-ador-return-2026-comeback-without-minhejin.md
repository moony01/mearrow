In late 2025, a South Korean court handed down its decision.

It sided with ADOR.

In the weeks that followed, all five members of NewJeans — Minji, Danielle, Haerin, Hanni, and Hyein — signaled their return to the label. Haerin and Hyein confirmed first. The other three followed. One member's announcement was delayed because she was reportedly in Antarctica at the time. Their legal representatives, unable to reach ADOR directly, issued a separate public statement.

Min Heejin — the creative director and former ADOR CEO who had conceived, designed, and built NewJeans from scratch — was gone. She founded her own record label, ooak records.

Now, in March 2026, ADOR is preparing a comeback. A full-length album is reportedly in development. The label says it began preparation regardless of the outcome of the lawsuit.

The question that the K-pop industry has not been able to set aside is this:

**What is NewJeans without Min Heejin?**

## The Person Who Designed Everything

NewJeans was not a standard K-pop group.

From their debut, NewJeans operated according to a specific and deliberately unusual philosophy. Musically, they drew from Y2K nostalgia, jersey club, UK garage, and newtro aesthetics — reference points that sat outside the mainstream K-pop production playbook. Visually, they chose naturalism over elaborate staging. In fandom culture, they pushed against the competitive hierarchy between members that most K-pop groups rely on, presenting all five members as equals.

The architect of that philosophy was Min Heejin.

As creative director and ADOR CEO, Min oversaw NewJeans' music direction, visual language, art design, and the way the group engaged with its fanbase. Her aesthetic choices became so identified with the group that industry analysts described NewJeans as "the grammar Min Heejin invented."

The results were real. Record-breaking streaming numbers from debut. An unusually strong reception in Western markets. A fanbase — BUNNIES — that organized around values that were distinctly different from most K-pop fandoms.

That person is now at a different label.

## What ADOR Is Preparing

After the court ruling, ADOR's official position has been consistent: the label has been preparing NewJeans' comeback independently of the lawsuit's resolution.

A full-length album is reportedly in development. Specific release dates have not been confirmed. The production team and creative direction for the new album have not been announced publicly.

In ordinary circumstances, a label preparing an album for a returning group is unremarkable. NewJeans are not ordinary circumstances.

This comeback has to do something specific: prove that ADOR and HYBE can run NewJeans without Min Heejin and still produce music that means something. That is a much higher bar than simply releasing an album.

The weight of that proof falls on the members as much as on the label.

## Three Challenges That Cannot Be Avoided

NewJeans' 2026 comeback faces three concrete challenges.

**First: the creative vacuum.** NewJeans' musical identity is built on a specific aesthetic. The person who designed that aesthetic is gone. Who leads the creative vision for the new album? In K-pop, changing a group's creative direction happens routinely. But for a group whose identity was so completely tied to one person's philosophy, the gap is not a personnel change — it is a foundational question. Fans will compare. Industry will judge.

**Second: the fractured fanbase.** BUNNIES spent the past year divided — those who supported Min Heejin's position and those who trusted HYBE and ADOR. The members' return does not automatically close that divide. Some fans welcomed the decision. Others have maintained that NewJeans without Min Heejin is not genuinely NewJeans. Whether this comeback reunifies the fanbase or hardens those divisions is one of the most important early signals to watch.

**Third: the reputational damage.** For more than a year, NewJeans occupied the center of corporate lawsuits, press conferences, and public battles involving adults making legal and business decisions. That the group's youngest members were in the middle of it, visible and on the record, is a separate wound from the music hiatus. Music alone may not close it.

## Min Heejin's ooak Records — A Variable No One Should Ignore

Min Heejin has not stepped back from the industry.

ooak records is her new label. She has not yet announced who she is working with or in what direction she is moving. But she has made clear she is not leaving K-pop.

If ooak records debuts an act — and if that act carries even some of the visual and musical language that defined NewJeans — the K-pop conversation around NewJeans' ADOR comeback becomes considerably more complicated. Fans and industry observers will have a direct point of comparison: what Min Heejin is doing now, versus what NewJeans is doing without her.

That is not hypothetical pressure. It is the actual structural situation going into this comeback.

## What MEARROW Is Watching

NewJeans' 2026 comeback has to prove three things simultaneously.

**That ADOR can run NewJeans without Min Heejin.** Rebuilding creative capacity, establishing a new aesthetic direction that is coherent — and convincing enough to be taken seriously by a skeptical audience.

**That NewJeans' members still have a musical identity of their own.** Separate from the corporate dispute, separate from the label, separate from who is or isn't directing their vision — can Minji, Danielle, Hanni, Haerin, and Hyein carry a comeback on their own terms?

**That BUNNIES can become one fanbase again.** A divided fandom has limited commercial and cultural energy. Reunification requires something worth reunifying around.

Failure on any one of these counts would be significant. Failure on all three would mark one of the sharper declines in K-pop's recent history.

Plenty of groups have survived label disputes. Few have survived the particular combination of factors that NewJeans now carries into a comeback: a year of public litigation, the departure of their defining creative force, and a divided fanbase watching both them and the person who made them.

2026 is where NewJeans either proves they were always more than one person's vision — or proves the opposite.
