The numbers from ARIRANG's first 72 hours are difficult to argue with.

3.98 million copies sold on release day — BTS breaking their own record before it was a week old. All 14 tracks on Melon's Top 100 simultaneously. Spotify's most-streamed album in a single day for all of 2026. The title track **"SWIM"** posting 14.6 million first-day streams, clearing Harry Styles' previous 2026 Spotify Daily Global record by over two million. 260,000 fans at Gwanghwamun for a comeback concert that Netflix streamed to 190 countries.

And yet one stat sits unchanged: **Grammy wins, zero.**

That number has haunted BTS since their first nomination in 2021. It remains untouched as ARIRANG dominates every chart that will have it. But something about this album is different — and the difference is visible in the credits.

## The Grammy Strategy Hidden in the Liner Notes

Among the 48 overseas production staff who contributed to ARIRANG, **eight are Grammy Award winners**.

The Korea Times, which reported this figure on March 23, described BTS's Grammy ambition as "clearly reflected in the lineup." That is an understatement. This is not a K-pop album that happened to cross over. This is an album that was structurally designed with the Grammy Academy in mind.

The producers are not named in available reporting, but the list of credited contributors to ARIRANG includes names across genres: **Diplo**, whose work spans dance music and pop crossover; **Tame Impala's Kevin Parker**, a critical darling whose collaborations attract prestige attention; **Mike WiLL Made-It**, a hip-hop producer with deep Grammy credibility; and **Ryan Tedder**, whose song placement on Grammy-contending albums is practically a career specialty. Eight of the forty-eight overseas contributors have already been in that room at the Staples Center, or the Crypto.com Arena, or wherever the ceremony happens to be held when K-pop finally gets its moment.

## What Suga Said in 2022

After BTS's second consecutive Grammy nomination — and second consecutive loss — Suga gave an interview that landed harder than most.

> "There's a saying that no tree remains standing after 10 blows of an ax, but hoping for a Grammy after only two attempts may be too much to ask."

Four years later, ARIRANG represents somewhere between the third and fifth serious attempt, depending on how you count. The group has been nominated for **Best Pop Duo/Group Performance** (2021, for "Dynamite") and again in 2022. Neither nomination produced a win.

Suga's framing in 2022 was humble and self-deprecating. But read alongside ARIRANG's production credits, it also reads like a diagnosis of the problem and a plan to solve it. If two attempts weren't enough, build the conditions to make the third — or fourth, or fifth — impossible to ignore.

## "SWIM": An English-Language Grammy Bid

The lead single is not an accident.

**"SWIM"** is sung entirely in English. It features **Lili Reinhart** — an American actress best known for *Riverdale* — in the music video. It was directed by **Tamu Muino**, who built her reputation directing Grammy-nominated videos for **Lil Nas X** and **Harry Styles**. The song topped the Spotify Daily Global chart for two consecutive days after release (March 20–21), racking up streams at a pace the platform called the most for any 2026 song to date.

Every element of "SWIM" signals Grammy strategy. The English lyrics remove the language barrier that some voters have historically cited — fairly or not — as a reason to hesitate. Reinhart's presence creates a pop-culture bridge to an American audience. Muino's direction gives the video a Grammy-category-eligible prestige sheen. If the Recording Academy is going to give BTS a Grammy, "SWIM" is engineered to make the case undeniable.

## The K-Pop Grammy Paradox

Here is the uncomfortable question at the center of this conversation: if BTS is the most commercially successful K-pop act in history, why has the Grammy Academy been unable to recognize them?

The numbers put this paradox in sharp relief. BTS holds more **Billboard Hot 100 #1 singles** than nearly any other group in the 2020s. They are the only act in history to have six or more non-consecutive #1 albums on the Billboard 200. Their fan base, ARMY, has been credited with transforming how the music industry understands digital streaming, social media mobilization, and global fan economics. If commercial success and cultural impact were the criteria, the Grammy would have come years ago.

But Grammy voting does not work on those criteria. The Recording Academy votes on music — and more specifically, on music as understood by the roughly 13,000 voting members, who skew heavily toward American industry professionals, session musicians, and producers across a range of genres. K-pop, despite its massive streaming numbers, occupies an uncomfortable position in that ecosystem: too commercially successful to be ignored, too stylistically distinct from American pop idioms to fit cleanly into any single category.

The Academy has also historically resisted creating a separate K-pop category, a decision that leaves Korean-language songs competing directly against American pop in categories where genre and language familiarity matter to voters. BTS has navigated this by increasingly working in English — "Dynamite," "Butter," "Permission to Dance," and now "SWIM" are all English-language tracks — but even those efforts have not converted nominations to wins.

## What Would a Win Actually Require?

**Best Pop Duo/Group Performance** has been BTS's most logical target, and it remains so with "SWIM." The field in a given year typically includes major Western pop acts. The difference ARIRANG makes is in the depth of Western production credibility behind it — Grammy-winner-level credibility that voters in that category recognize by name and reputation.

**Album of the Year** is a longer shot, but not a dismissible one. ARIRANG will almost certainly be one of the highest-selling and highest-streaming albums of 2026 globally. Whether the Academy's historical resistance to non-American albums bends depends partly on the competitive landscape in January 2027.

**Best Music Video** is arguably the most plausible win vector for "SWIM," given Tamu Muino's track record with nominated videos and the song's production values.

Industry observers quoted in Korean media have flagged another variable: the Grammy season runs on a different timeline than K-pop's moment. ARIRANG releases in March 2026. Grammy consideration would be for the 2027 ceremony. A lot can change in ten months — including whether the broader cultural conversation about K-pop's place in American music continues to intensify.

## What MEARROW Is Watching

The Grammy story around BTS is not new. It has been running since 2021, refreshed each awards season with a new cycle of nominations and a new round of losses. What is new with ARIRANG is the intentionality.

BTS has made K-pop Grammy bids before. They have not made a Grammy bid quite like this one — where the engineering is this visible in the credits, this embedded in the lead single's casting and direction, this deliberate in its deployment of every Western industry lever available.

Suga's 2022 quote about the ax and the tree is worth revisiting with this context. The ARIRANG production team may be providing ten blows in a single album.

Whether the tree falls is a question for January 2027. Right now, the numbers are what they are: 3.98 million copies, Spotify records, global charts, and a Grammy count that hasn't moved from zero since BTS began trying.

Something has to give eventually. BTS, at minimum, has made it structurally harder than ever to explain why it shouldn't.
