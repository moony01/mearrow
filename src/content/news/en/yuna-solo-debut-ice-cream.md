Yeji's solo album was called AIR. Dark, heavy electro beats carrying a voice that declared independence. One year later, the youngest member of the same group stepped forward with 'Ice Cream.' Bubblegum pop, hip-hop, R&B, disco — the polar opposite in every way. Can two solos from the same JYP group really diverge this dramatically? The short answer: this wasn't an accident. It was by design.

## Yeji's AIR — Finding Darkness Outside the Group

### The Dark Electro Gamble

In March 2025, Yeji became ITZY's first solo artist with her mini-album AIR. The title track 'Air' layered vulnerability over sharp, dark electro beats. Critics responded enthusiastically — NME named it one of the best K-pop songs of 2025. B-sides 'Invasion,' 'Can't Slow Me,' and '258' maintained a consistently somber tone throughout.

### The Chart Report Card

It missed the Billboard 200. The album landed at No. 16 on Top Current Album Sales, No. 6 on World Albums, and Yeji debuted at No. 73 on the Billboard Artist 100 — alongside LE SSERAFIM, Jennie, and Rosé. Respectable company, but the numbers themselves weren't explosive.

For JYP, Yeji's AIR was a debut that delivered critical acclaim while leaving commercial questions on the table.

## Yuna's Ice Cream — Sweet Subversion

### The Youngest Chooses the Opposite Path

On March 23, 2026, Yuna released her mini-album 'Ice Cream.' The title track is bright, addictive bubblegum pop with a message about living freely in the moment — "like ice cream melting away."

### A Genre Spectrum in 4 Tracks

The tracklist reveals the key difference from Yeji's approach.

| Track | Genre | Mood |
|-------|-------|------|
| Ice Cream (Title) | Bubblegum Pop | Bright, catchy |
| B-Boy | Hip-Hop | Bold, confident |
| Blue Maze | R&B | Dreamy, delicate |
| Hyper Dream | Disco | Energetic, retro |

Four tracks spanning pop, hip-hop, R&B, and disco. In a single mini-album, Yuna shattered the formula that "an idol member's solo means one genre." Hypebae described her as "confident, versatile, and fully in command of her moment."

## Same JYP, Opposite Strategies — Not a Coincidence

### The Blueprint of a Dual-Track Experiment

Place ITZY's two solo projects side by side and the pattern becomes clear.

| Category | Yeji's AIR (Mar 2025) | Yuna's Ice Cream (Mar 2026) |
|----------|----------------------|----------------------------|
| Tone | Dark, heavy | Bright, playful |
| Primary Genre | Dark electro | Bubblegum pop |
| Genre Range | Narrow (consistent darkness) | Wide (4 genres) |
| Title Track Language | Korean-based | English title |
| Distribution | JYP Entertainment | JYP + Imperial + Republic Records |

Yuna's 'Ice Cream' is distributed through Imperial and Republic Records — a global distribution partnership that didn't exist for Yeji's AIR. JYP laid a wider global pipeline for Yuna's solo.

### JYP's Math

This isn't simply a story of "different members, different music." After Yeji's AIR earned critical praise but couldn't crack the Billboard 200, JYP played the opposite card with Yuna. Bright, accessible pop sound. Multi-genre composition. Enhanced global distribution. The strategy is clear: compare market responses from both solos to find the optimal direction for ITZY's solo lineup.

At the same time, JYP's other flagship act Stray Kids announced a farewell tour ahead of military enlistment. JYP needs ITZY members' solo activities to fill the Stray Kids vacuum. The precise one-year spacing between Yeji and Yuna's solos reads clearly in this context.

## MEARROW's Take on the ITZY Solo Experiment

MEARROW views ITZY's solo projects not as simple individual activities, but as JYP's **portfolio rebalancing strategy**.

The 4th-generation K-pop girl group market in 2026 is extremely crowded. aespa, LE SSERAFIM, NewJeans, IVE, and (G)I-DLE are locked in fierce group-level competition, while the solo market remains relatively untapped. Rosé and Jennie proved the "4th-gen soloist" model viable by charting on the Billboard Hot 100 without the BLACKPINK name.

JYP testing artistry with Yeji and mainstream appeal with Yuna isn't about not knowing which approach works. It's about **trying both and letting the data decide**. The solo directions for remaining members Lia, Ryujin, and Chaeryeong will be shaped by these two results.

## The Next Chapter Begins

Whether Yuna's 'Ice Cream' commercially surpasses Yeji's AIR is the immediate question. But the bigger one lies ahead: will ITZY itself be redefined as "the sum of its solo parts"?

JYP already walked this path with TWICE. Nayeon, Jihyo, Tzuyu, and Chaeyoung each released solo albums, transforming TWICE into a group that's "strong together, strong apart." Can the same formula apply to ITZY? Somewhere between Yeji's darkness and Yuna's sugar lies the answer.
