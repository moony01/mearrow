`NewJeans Minji return` rumors did not restart with a comeback poster, a press conference, or a clean agency timetable. They restarted with cookies. On May 7, ADOR said discussions about Minji's future activities were moving in a positive direction, the same day NewJeans' official channels acknowledged her birthday with images connected to cookies she had prepared for fans. The easy read is that NewJeans may be closer to a four-member return. The sharper read is that ADOR has started testing whether a soft personal signal can survive one of K-pop's most damaged trust environments. ## NewJeans Minji Return Rumors Start With A Cookie

### ADOR chose the gentlest possible signal

Korea JoongAng Daily, AJU Press and Soompi all reported the same core line: ADOR said it was continuing internal discussions over Minji's future activities and described the talks as generally moving in a positive direction. That phrasing is careful. It does not confirm a return date, a lineup, a song, or a schedule. It gives fans just enough movement to read, while protecting the company from promising more than it can deliver.

That is why the cookie post matters. NewJeans' official social media accounts posted images tied to Minji's birthday, after reports that she had visited fan-organized birthday cafe events and left handwritten letters and homemade cookies. Korea JoongAng Daily described it as the first time Minji had been mentioned on an official NewJeans account since the group entered its legal dispute with ADOR in November 2024. For a normal group, a birthday post is routine. For NewJeans, it behaves like a public-status signal.

### The public saw timing before wording

The sequence gave fans something concrete to debate. First came the birthday cafe story. Then came the official account post. Then came ADOR's cautious comment. None of those pieces alone equals a comeback announcement, but together they created a pattern that felt deliberate.

That is the real hook. ADOR did not need to stage a hard statement if the goal was only to reduce speculation. A hard statement would have forced the company to say yes, no, or not yet. Instead, the label let a warmer object carry part of the message. Cookies and letters are emotionally safer than legal language, especially for a group whose recent history has been written through injunctions, contract validity rulings, and public mistrust.

## The Four-Member Question Is Now Back On The Table

### NewJeans is not operating from a clean lineup

Soompi reported that Haerin and Hyein returned to ADOR last November, Hanni returned in December, Danielle has been notified of the termination of her exclusive contract, and Minji's return remains under discussion. Korea JoongAng Daily also framed Minji's status as unclear after a court ruled in ADOR's favor last October in the contract dispute.

That makes the current signal bigger than a single member update. If Minji and ADOR reach an agreement, NewJeans could move toward a four-member structure. If they do not, ADOR's next musical chapter may have to proceed with a more sharply reduced lineup. Neither path restores the original five-member image that defined the group's early identity.

### Copenhagen made the gap visible

StarNews reported in late April that Hanni, Haerin and Hyein visited Copenhagen with staff as part of a pre-production process for a new musical narrative. The same report said Minji did not participate in that schedule. That detail gives the May 7 cookie signal a sharper edge.

If the Copenhagen activity was early comeback preparation, then Minji's absence was not just a calendar footnote. It was the visible gap in the room. The birthday post does not erase that gap, but it changes how fans read it. Before May 7, the question was whether ADOR was preparing to move around Minji. After May 7, the question became whether ADOR is leaving space for her to re-enter.

## Why ADOR Needed A Soft Signal First

### A hard announcement would trigger the whole dispute again

NewJeans is not returning from a normal hiatus. The group is returning, if it returns, through the debris of the HYBE-ADOR-Min Hee-jin conflict, a court fight over exclusive contracts, Danielle's exit, and months of fan argument over who betrayed whom. In that context, any direct statement about Minji risks becoming a referendum on the entire dispute.

The cookie signal works differently. It lets ADOR acknowledge Minji through fandom emotion rather than corporate victory language. It gives Bunnies a human image before it gives them a business answer. That matters because NewJeans' strongest brand asset was never only music. It was intimacy: the feeling that the members, visuals, sound, and fan relationship belonged to one unusually coherent world.

### Soft does not mean accidental

The softer the object, the easier it is to underestimate the strategy behind it. A birthday post can look harmless, but official-channel visibility is never neutral inside a disputed artist structure. The company controls what appears on those accounts. Fans know that. Journalists know that. Other labels know that.

So the post becomes a low-risk market test. If fans respond with relief, ADOR learns that a Minji path can still create emotional lift. If fans respond with suspicion, ADOR learns that a future comeback announcement will need much stronger framing. Either way, the company gets information before it has to publish a timetable.

## How The Public Is Reading The Signal

### Minji's name now carries two stories at once

The current public reading of Minji is split. One side sees her birthday cafe visit and cookie gifts as a sincere bridge back to fans after a long period of uncertainty. Another side reads every official post through the legal dispute and asks whether the label is using warmth to prepare the ground for a business decision already made behind closed doors.

That split will shape reception even if the facts remain simple. A return announcement would not only be judged as "Minji is back." It would be judged as proof, compromise, pressure, healing, surrender, or strategy depending on which side of the fandom is reading it.

At the same time, Minji's present-day public perception does not decide the truth of the 2024 dispute. The legal and historical facts of that conflict remain a separate layer from how fans feel about her now. The important point for this story is reception: the way current sympathy, skepticism, and exhaustion will determine whether a NewJeans comeback feels like recovery or damage control.

## MEARROW's View On The ADOR Cookie Signal

### NewJeans now has to relaunch trust before music

MEARROW reads this as a trust rehearsal. ADOR may eventually need a comeback announcement, track list, concept photos, and a release date, but the first product being tested is not a song. It is permission. Can the label talk about Minji on official NewJeans channels without triggering immediate rejection? Can fans accept a four-member future if the fifth-member image is gone? Can a group built on naturalness return through a process that now feels heavily managed?

Those questions are commercial, not only emotional. A NewJeans comeback would need streaming, album sales, brand confidence, media framing, and fan labor. If the fandom enters the rollout angry or divided, every teaser becomes harder to interpret. If the fandom enters with cautious relief, ADOR has a runway.

### The cookie was small because the stakes are not

That is why the May 7 signal should not be dismissed as cute fan service. It is small precisely because the stakes are large. ADOR cannot rebuild NewJeans' public image in one statement, and Minji cannot resolve the whole dispute through one birthday gesture. But soft signals can reopen a door that legal statements kept slamming shut.

The next move will matter more than the post itself. If ADOR follows with a clear update, the cookie signal becomes the first breadcrumb in a comeback path. If silence returns, it becomes another artifact for fans to overread. For now, the strongest conclusion is also the most uncomfortable one: NewJeans may be closer to movement, but movement is not the same thing as repair.
