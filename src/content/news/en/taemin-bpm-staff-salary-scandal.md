On February 24, a single announcement shook the K-pop world.

"We would like to inform you that our exclusive contract with Taemin has concluded."

The statement came from Big Planet Made Entertainment (BPM). Despite time remaining on the contract, Taemin had strongly requested termination — and the company had no choice but to accept. Then, almost immediately after the announcement, an even more startling detail emerged: Taemin had been **personally paying his staff's salaries** out of his own pocket, because his agency couldn't.

## The 18 Billion Won Debt Nobody Talked About

At the center of this crisis is BPM CEO Cha Ga Won, who also heads One Hundred and INB100. The full scale of the problem first came to light on February 5, through an exclusive investigation by The Fact.

According to the report, the companies under Cha's leadership had failed to pay dozens of vendors essential to artist operations — album production, content creation, hair and makeup, marketing, and even stage set fees for music show appearances. One industry insider, identified only as Mr. A, described the fallout:

> "I did nothing wrong, yet I became a debtor. I became a liar to the people who trusted me. My mental state collapsed. Months later, my bank account was seized for unpaid health insurance. I have a child. I sold my car and took out loans just to cover basic expenses."

BPM's 2024 financial report had already been flashing warning signs. The company's liabilities exceeded its assets by 18 billion won. Yet contracts with artists continued, and activities went on as if nothing was wrong.

### Taemin Wasn't Alone

The artists caught in this crisis extend far beyond Taemin. Baekhyun, Xiumin, Chen (EXO), and The Boyz — all under Cha's companies — are reportedly owed billions of won in unpaid settlements. Their activities have been effectively paralyzed: vendors who weren't paid began refusing to provide services, making normal operations impossible.

## The Moment Taemin Opened His Own Wallet

According to Sports Chosun, as BPM's payment failures mounted, Taemin stepped in and personally covered a portion of his staff's wages. With the company unable to pay, and his team facing financial hardship, Taemin chose to act.

What makes this even more striking: according to AsiaOne, BPM owed Taemin himself over 1 billion won in unpaid wages. He was paying his staff out of his own pocket while the company owed him money.

### Two Reasons the Contract Ended

Taemin's grounds for termination came down to two issues. First, the unpaid settlements. Second — and more legally decisive — Cha Ga Won had allegedly signed a contract with an external company without Taemin's consent. This was a direct violation of the exclusive contract's core terms, and it gave Taemin clear legal standing to demand termination.

An industry insider noted that "CEO Cha had no grounds to refuse Taemin's request." With time still remaining on the contract, BPM accepted the termination.

## What This Reveals About K-pop Contracts

This isn't just a story about one agency's financial mismanagement. It's a window into how K-pop's contract structure continues to leave artists exposed.

Under standard exclusive contracts, artists cannot easily exit even when an agency is in financial distress. Unilateral termination during the contract period typically triggers penalty clauses. It's only when an agency commits a clear contractual violation — as in Taemin's case — that legal termination becomes viable.

The deeper issue is **settlement transparency**. Artists often have limited visibility into how much they've earned and when they'll be paid. Cha Ga Won's situation is an extreme case, but the structural vulnerability it exposes is industry-wide.

## MEARROW's Take

MEARROW sees three signals in this story.

**First, artists are increasingly willing to protect themselves.** Taemin pushed for contract termination despite time remaining on the deal. This is a meaningful shift from the era when K-pop idols largely deferred to their agencies, even under unfair conditions. It reflects a growing maturity in artist rights awareness — a slow but real evolution since the JYJ era.

**Second, agency financial health needs independent verification.** BPM's 2024 financials showed liabilities exceeding assets. Artists signing with agencies should have access to independent financial due diligence before committing. The industry needs structural safeguards, not just legal remedies after the fact.

**Third, Taemin's actions set a standard.** Paying staff out of his own pocket — while the company owed him money — isn't just a heartwarming story. It's a statement about what kind of artist he is: someone who treats his team as people, not just resources. In an industry that often reduces artists to products, that matters.

## The Questions That Remain

Taemin has not yet signed with a new agency. He is scheduled to perform at Coachella in April, making his next move one of the most watched in K-pop right now.

Baekhyun, Xiumin, Chen, and The Boyz remain under Cha's companies. Whether their unpaid settlements will be resolved — and whether this crisis will finally push the industry toward meaningful contract reform — those are the questions that matter most.
