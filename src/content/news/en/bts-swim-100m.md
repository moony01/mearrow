## The first real headline is the 26-day clock

### Released on March 20, past 100 million on April 15

According to Soompi, the music video for `SWIM` crossed 100 million YouTube views at around 1 p.m. KST on April 15, 2026. That means it took exactly 26 days from its March 20, 1 p.m. KST release, making it the first K-pop music video released in 2026 to hit that mark. The bigger point is not the milestone alone. After a long gap in full-group activity, BTS has shown that the audience speed around a new release still has almost no slack in it.

### This is not just a video stat, it is a live measure of mobilization

Music-video views are no longer just ceremonial trophies. Fandoms now move in phases: purchase first, stream heavily, then return to video and push the narrative back into public view. `SWIM` reaching 100 million in 26 days suggests BTS fans are still operating across platforms instead of burning all of their energy in week one. That matters because it points to stamina, not just excitement. Short bursts create headlines. Stamina creates quarters.

## What makes it bigger is that YouTube is not acting alone

### Billboard and Spotify kept moving in the same direction

This story reads differently because the YouTube spike matches what is happening elsewhere. The Korea Times and Soompi reported that `ARIRANG` held No. 1 on the Billboard 200 for a third straight week dated April 13, the first time a K-pop act has done that. In the prior week, the album had already stayed atop the chart with 187,000 equivalent units. Spotify moved the same way. The Korea Times reported that BTS spent 14 straight days at No. 1 on both the Daily Top Artists Global and Daily Top Songs Global charts starting March 20. That is not a one-song flare-up. It is brand-level pressure.

### When the floor stays high, the market keeps listening

Many K-pop comebacks post huge first-week numbers and then split apart by platform. `ARIRANG` opened with 641,000 equivalent units, then still held 187,000 in its second week while remaining at No. 1. The point is not that the drop disappeared. It is that the floor stayed high enough for the story to keep traveling. When a release can hold sales, maintain streaming weight, and then convert that momentum into video acceleration, the public conversation stops being about a launch and starts becoming about sustained market dominance.

Tour clips spread, those clips send attention back to the official video, and the official video pushes people back into streaming and social chatter.
