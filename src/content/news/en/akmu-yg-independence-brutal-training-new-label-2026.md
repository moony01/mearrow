At some point in 2025, Yang Hyun-suk — YG Entertainment's founding executive producer and the most powerful individual in the company's history — personally showed up at Lee Chan-hyuk and Lee Suhyun's home.

YG confirmed this themselves: "About six months before the contract expiration, Yang Hyun-suk visited the home where Chan-hyuk and Suhyun live, and shared a meal together. They had a candid conversation about the concerns AKMU had been holding."

Six months later, AKMU left.

## How You End a 12-Year Relationship

### Why Yang Visited in Person

An executive producer showing up at an artist's home is not standard industry procedure. Especially not when a contract renewal is on the line.

That detail matters. YG didn't send an email. They didn't schedule a meeting at the office. The most powerful man at the label went to their house. That's how much YG wanted to keep them.

And AKMU still left.

In November 2025, YG officially announced the end of their exclusive contract with AKMU. The tone of the statement was notably different from most K-pop label separations — which tend to either be abrupt or politically charged.

"We were aware of the upcoming contract expiration approximately six months in advance and had frank conversations with both about their future direction. Respecting AKMU's musical aspirations and desire for independence, both parties have mutually agreed to part ways amicably."

By K-pop standards, that's as clean a separation as it gets.

### What 12 Years Actually Means

AKMU debuted in 2013 after winning Mnet's *Superstar K 5*. They never changed agencies. Not once. For twelve years, they were YG.

To understand what twelve years means in this industry: when AKMU joined YG, BTS had just debuted. BLACKPINK didn't exist. K-pop hadn't broken into the American mainstream. Streaming was in its infancy.

Through all of that — AKMU stayed. And now they've left.

## 'Center of Inspiration' — The Name as a Manifesto

### An Independent Label Built from Scratch

On January 20, 2026, AKMU officially announced their new agency: **영감의 샘터**, which translates to **Center of Inspiration**.

Chan-hyuk designed the logo himself. It looked hand-drawn — modest, personal, deliberately unpolished. The aesthetic contrast with YG's slick corporate branding was impossible to miss.

The name is itself a statement. "We make music from inspiration. And now that space belongs to us."

The official contract transfer date was January 27, 2026. From that moment, AKMU became fully independent artists.

### What Independence Means in K-pop

K-pop's infrastructure is built around the Big 4. Training, marketing, distribution, global expansion — all of it flows through HYBE, SM, JYP, or YG. Leaving that system means leaving behind access to those pipelines.

AKMU's bet is that they don't need them. And that bet isn't without basis. For twelve years inside YG, AKMU's music was never really YG's music. '200%', 'How Can I Love the Heartbreak, You're the One I Love', 'Adolescence' — those songs came from Chan-hyuk's brain, not from the label's production apparatus.

The question is whether that advantage holds when the rest of the infrastructure disappears.

## January 1st: The "Brutal Training" Declaration

### Chan-hyuk's New Year's Statement

On January 1, 2026 — the first day of the new year, weeks before the formal label launch — Lee Chan-hyuk posted on Weverse.

"AKMU has been going through brutal training since January 1st in preparation for a new start."

It was a short post. Fans understood it immediately. The moment AKMU stepped out of one of Korea's most powerful entertainment companies, Chan-hyuk announced they were treating themselves like rookies again.

Not coasting on twelve years of credibility. Not resting. Restarting from scratch — with the intensity of debut preparation.

### Day 8: Suhyun's Tears

Eight days into the training, Lee Suhyun cried.

The specific reason wasn't disclosed publicly. From what fans gathered, the weight of the training itself — or the emotional enormity of what they'd chosen to do — became too much in that moment.

The thing is: that tells you something real. "Brutal training" wasn't performance language. It was description.

Which raises the obvious question: Why leave the security of one of K-pop's founding labels for this?

## A 4th Studio Album — Seven Years in the Making

### The Last One Was in 2019

AKMU's third studio album, *항해* (Voyage), released in 2019. In the years since: mini-albums, singles, collaborations. But no studio album.

In February 2026, AKMU teased their upcoming fourth studio album. Seven years since the last one.

Suddenly, the brutal training has a destination.

### What the First Independent Album Actually Means

This isn't just the next release in a discography. It's AKMU's first record made entirely on their own terms — without a major label's infrastructure, without a team of executives in the approval chain, without a marketing department making campaign decisions.

Chan-hyuk writes and produces everything. That was true at YG too. But now the timeline, the creative direction, the rollout, the deals — all of it is theirs.

| Category | Under YG | Center of Inspiration |
|----------|---------|------------|
| Creative direction | Chan-hyuk led, YG review | Fully autonomous |
| Marketing | YG system | Self-operated |
| Distribution | YG network | Independent contracts |
| Global reach | YG infrastructure | Being rebuilt |

The trade is obvious: resources for freedom. The question is whether it's worth it.

## MEARROW's Read on AKMU's Gamble

AKMU's independence move is unusual in K-pop — and it's only possible because of what AKMU uniquely is.

They're not an idol group. They're a songwriter-performer duo. Chan-hyuk composes and writes. Suhyun performs. The label doesn't build the music; Chan-hyuk does. That's why leaving a major label doesn't automatically mean losing the creative product. The product was always coming from them.

But distribution, marketing, and global reach — those are real losses. AKMU will have to rebuild those pipelines from scratch, or find partners who can provide them.

That's why the brutal training and the Day-8 tears matter as data points, not just human interest moments. They show that AKMU is treating this with the seriousness it demands. They're not walking out of YG into a comfortable creative sabbatical. They're starting over.

**The name they chose — Center of Inspiration — tells you what they believe:** that great music comes from being moved by something, and that the best environment for that is one they control.

Whether that belief translates into their strongest work yet, or whether the absence of a major label's scaffolding becomes a ceiling — that's what the fourth studio album will answer.

After twelve years, AKMU is making their first truly independent record. Yang Hyun-suk came to their house, had dinner, and still couldn't stop them.

MEARROW will be watching.
