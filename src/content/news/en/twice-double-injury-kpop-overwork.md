"I really don't know why these things only happen to me." When TWICE's Jeongyeon posted these words to fans, the K-pop world paused. Her first and only brand deal in over a decade with JYP Entertainment had been canceled without explanation. She laughed it off, but fans didn't. And before that anger could settle, two of her groupmates went down — one after another — in the middle of TWICE's 43-city world tour.

## A Chair on Stage — Dahyun's Month of Endurance

### The Crack That Started in Vancouver

January 9, 2026. Vancouver. The North American leg of TWICE's "THIS IS FOR" world tour kicked off with all nine members on stage. But as shows progressed, something was visibly wrong with Dahyun. By the Seattle show on January 12, she was completing multiple segments seated in a chair. Her ankle had given out.

Photos of Dahyun performing while seated spread across social media. But JYP Entertainment said nothing. Dahyun continued receiving treatment while touring the U.S. — on a fractured ankle.

### A Diagnosis That Came a Month Too Late

On February 11, JYP finally issued a statement: "Dahyun experienced ankle discomfort during the early stages of the tour. After returning to Korea and undergoing detailed examinations, she was diagnosed with a fracture." She would sit out from February 13 through March 7.

Fans were furious. Dahyun had been performing on a chair since January, yet it took a full month for an official diagnosis. The question echoed across fan communities: "How do you not know your artist has a broken bone for an entire month?"

March 7 came and went. Dahyun didn't return. On March 25, JYP released a second statement: "After carefully discussing the artist's condition and her ability to participate in performances, we have determined that rest and recovery should take priority." Dahyun's tour was effectively over.

## Chaeyoung Goes Down Next — The Other Side of 43 Cities

### Vasovagal Syncope, Then a Back Injury

The day after Dahyun's second withdrawal announcement — March 26 — another member fell. This time it was Chaeyoung. JYP's statement: "While preparing for departure, Chaeyoung suddenly experienced an issue with her lower back this morning. Urgent medical attention was required, and her departure schedule has been inevitably postponed."

This wasn't Chaeyoung's first health crisis. In November 2025, she was diagnosed with vasovagal syncope — a condition that causes sudden drops in blood pressure and heart rate, leading to fainting. She missed shows in Kaohsiung, Hong Kong, and Bangkok, and halted all activities through year-end. Just three months after returning, her body broke down again.

### From Nine to Seven

TWICE is a nine-member group. As of March 27, 2026, only seven members are performing. The 43-city world tour isn't even halfway done. A solo concert at Tokyo's National Stadium is scheduled for April. The tour doesn't stop.

## Fans Erupted. JYP Went Silent.

### "That's Just Straight-Up Negligence" — ONCE Speaks

TWICE's fandom, ONCE, directed fierce criticism at JYP Entertainment. Three issues dominated the conversation.

First, the **delayed diagnosis**. Photos of Dahyun performing seated surfaced in January, but the official fracture announcement didn't come until February 11. "You let her perform on a broken ankle for a month?" was the prevailing sentiment.

Second, the **recurring pattern**. Chaeyoung had collapsed from vasovagal syncope just four months earlier. Returning her to a 43-city tour after three months raised serious questions about whether adequate recovery time was provided.

Third, **Jeongyeon's canceled brand deal**. Her only brand partnership in a decade was scrapped due to scheduling failures. Jeongyeon herself said: "I'm really upset about it, too. Just like ONCE, I feel the same way." Trust in the agency's management capabilities is eroding.

## MEARROW's Perspective: K-pop's Overwork Machine

### The Tour That Never Stops

K-pop world tours have expanded aggressively since 2023. BTS's Arirang World Tour spans 82+ shows across 34 cities. TWICE's THIS IS FOR covers 43. As scale grows, schedules tighten, and idol bodies become expendable.

The problem is structural. Tour dates are locked months in advance, intertwined with venue bookings, ticket sales, and sponsor contracts. Canceling shows for one member isn't an option in this system. That's why Dahyun sat in a chair and performed on a broken ankle. That's why Chaeyoung joined a 43-city tour with a history of fainting spells.

MEARROW views this not as a JYP-specific failure but as a systemic limitation across the K-pop industry. A system where tour schedules take precedence over idol health. A system that only pauses when fans protest loudly enough. Unless this structure changes, what happened to TWICE will happen again.

## Seven Members. The Show Goes On.

On March 27, TWICE's tour resumed in Orlando, USA. Seven members took the stage. Dahyun's spot and Chaeyoung's spot were filled by adjusted formations.

ONCE is asking: who falls next? The tour pushes on toward Tokyo's National Stadium in April. The K-pop machine doesn't stop. Not until someone else goes down.
